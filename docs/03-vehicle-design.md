# 3. Vehicle design (airframe, mass, stability)

![Vehicle layout](img/vehicle_layout.svg)

## 3.1 Geometry

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Overall length | 60 in (1.524 m) |
| Body | LOC Precision 3.9 in kraft tube (3.9 in ID / 4.0 in OD), 43.5 in long |
| Nose | 3D-printed PETG ogive, 12.75 in exposed + 3.75 in shoulder (or a LOC 3.9 in plastic nose, ~142 g, at +47 g) |
| Fins | 4 x 1/8 in birch ply: root 4.7 in, tip 2.4 in, span 3.0 in, sweep 2.3 in, trailing edge flush with the aft end |
| Legs | 4 x 0.60 m carbon tube, hinged at 1.40 m station, deploy to 55° → 1.08 m tip-to-tip footprint |
| Motors | 2 x AeroTech G40W (29 x 124 mm) side by side in one gimbal, 33 mm centre-to-centre |

Why 4 inches? Two 29 mm motors side by side, plus ±7° of gimbal swing, need an
envelope of about 85 mm. A 3 in tube is 76 mm inside, so it doesn't fit.

- **Margin:** 113 g to the 1500 g model-rocket limit. Mass growth is the classic
  failure of TVC projects, so weigh as you go.
- **Lightening options if you are over:**
  - LOC carbon ultra-light tube instead of kraft (saves ~150 g, costs ~$300);
  - a lighter 2S 300 mAh pack;
  - drop the GPS and rely on IMU-only lateral velocity (costs landing accuracy);
  - thinner fins.
- **Pitch inertia:** 0.238 kg·m² at liftoff (computed). **Measure it** with a
  bifilar pendulum before tuning the TVC.

## 3.3 Aerodynamics and stability - the critical issue

A propulsive lander must be stable in **two** directions:

| Phase | Flow direction | What stabilises it | Static margin (calibers) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boost (motor on) | nose-first | TVC (+ fins) | +0.63 |
| Coast up (motor off, ~4 s) | nose-first | **fins** (TVC has no authority) | **+0.78** |
| Fall, no drogue | tail-first | nothing: fins at the leading end destabilise | **-4.31** (tumbles) |
| Fall with drogue | tail-first | **drogue on the nose** | **+1.24** |
| Landing burn | tail-first, slowing | TVC + drogue | (active) |

The CP model is component build-up:

- **Nose cone:** Barrowman ogive, CNα = 2 at 0.466 of the nose length, only
  when it leads the flow.
- **Blunt aft end:** CNα ≈ 1 when it leads.
- **Fins:** Barrowman, same in both directions.
- **Drogue:** C_d·S/A, acting at the nose.
- **Body:** viscous crossflow drag.

The model is in `sim/rocketsim/vehicle.py`. Barrowman background:
[Nakka](https://www.nakka-rocketry.net/RD_Appendix_B.html),
[OpenRocket tech doc](https://openrocket.sourceforge.net/techdoc.pdf).

**Why the drogue matters.** The vehicle is tail-heavy: the CG is at 1.03 m out
of 1.52 m because the motors, gimbal and legs are aft. A tail-heavy body falls
like a shuttlecock only if there is drag behind it (at the top). Falcon 9 gets
that from grid fins at the top of the stage. We get it from a 9 in drogue. The
drogue also:

- sets a near-constant **22.5 m/s** terminal speed. That speed is matched to
  the G40W landing burn, and the burn always starts from that state, so the
  ignition decision is insensitive to apogee scatter;
- keeps the vehicle tail-down while the paddles throttle the motor low;
- is cut away **at touchdown**, so ground wind can't drag the landed rocket
  over.

Note that **OpenRocket assumes nose-first flight**. Use it for mass, CG and
drag, not for the tail-first phase.

### Terminal velocity check

v_t = sqrt(2 m g / (ρ C_d A)):

- **No drogue:** C_A ≈ 1.07 tail-first, A = 8.1e-3 m², m = 1.33 kg →
  **v_t ≈ 50 m/s**. After a 150 m fall that is ~42 m/s, too fast for the G40W.
- **With the drogue:** C_d·S = 0.033 m² → **v_t ≈ 22.5 m/s**, which is the
  value the G40W is sized for.

## 3.4 Structure and materials

| Part | Material | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Body tube | LOC kraft 3.9 in (103 g/ft) | Lightest practical option. Reinforce the aft 6 in internally with a coupler or CF sleeve where the legs, fins and gimbal frame load it. ([LOC tubes](https://locprecision.com/products/cardboard-airframes), [tube mass comparison](https://fabacademy.org/2025/labs/techworks/students/omar-albalbaki/Body%20Tubes%201c70e8ca7ada81a6b85ddbe39d7d69ba.html)) |
| Nose | PETG, 0.8 mm walls, 15% gyroid | Tip has a 40 mm drogue cup with a spring and a servo latch |
| Bulkheads, centering rings | 1/8 in birch ply, or printed PETG/CF-nylon | Motor-bay thrust bulkhead: **ply + aluminium thrust plate**, since the motor thrust goes through the gimbal into it |
| Gimbal | CF-nylon / PETG printed rings, **aluminium motor sleeves**, steel pins / small bearings | PLA softens at ~60 °C; don't use it near the motor ([PETG vs PLA](https://www.makeitfrom.com/compare/Glycol-Modified-Polyethylene-Terephthalate-PETG-PET-G/Polylactic-Acid-PLA-Polylactide)). BPS needed a machined aluminium mount ([scifi.radio](https://scifi.radio/2022/08/07/meet-the-diy-rocketeer-who-can-copy-spacex/)). |
| Paddles | Machinable ceramic (alumina-silicate) or graphite on stainless arms | Expect them to erode; inspect and replace after 2-3 burns ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/one-mans-dream-to-build-a-working-spacex-style-model-rocket/)) |
| Fins | 1/8 in birch ply, epoxy fillets | Through-the-wall into the reinforced aft section |
| Legs | 8 mm OD carbon tube, PETG/CF-nylon hinge clevises, TPU feet | See [docs/07](07-landing-legs.md) |
| Adhesive | 30-minute epoxy (or West System 105/206 + 406 silica) for structure; high-temperature epoxy near the motors; 5-minute epoxy only for tacking | |

## 3.5 Launch hardware

- **Pad:** flat steel blast plate with a 3-point "launch clamp" or a 1.5 m guide
  rod. Clear this with the RSO (see [docs/02](02-regulations-and-safety.md#23-nar-model-rocket-safety-code-the-rules-your-club-will-enforce)).
  The rocket moves ~11 m/s after 1.5 m of travel.
- **Landing pad (optional):** a 2 x 2 m sheet of plywood near the launch pad
  gives the feet and the lidar a flat, reflective target.
