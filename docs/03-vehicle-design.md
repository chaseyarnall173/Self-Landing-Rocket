# 3. Vehicle design (airframe, mass, stability)

![Vehicle layout](img/vehicle_layout.svg)

## 3.1 Geometry

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Overall length | 60 in (1.524 m) |
| Body | LOC Precision 3.9 in kraft tube (3.9 in ID / 4.0 in OD), 43.5 in long |
| Nose | 3D-printed PETG ogive: 12.75 in exposed + 3.75 in shoulder (a LOC 3.9 in plastic nose, ~142 g, works at +47 g) |
| Top airbrakes | 4 petals cut from the body tube between stations 0.42 and 0.545 m (one quadrant each, ~80 x 125 mm), hinged at the forward edge |
| Fins | 4 x 1/8 in birch ply: root 4.7 in, tip 2.4 in, span 3.0 in, sweep 2.3 in; trailing edge flush with the aft end |
| Legs | 4 x 0.60 m carbon tube, hinged at station 1.40 m, deploying to 55° for a 1.08 m tip-to-tip footprint |
| Motors | AeroTech F42T (29 x 83 mm) + AeroTech F25W (29 x 98 mm) side by side in one gimbal, 33 mm centre-to-centre |

**Why 4 inches?** Two 29 mm motors side by side, plus ±7° of gimbal swing,
need an envelope of about 85 mm. A 3 in tube is only 76 mm inside.

## 3.3 Stability: coasting up and falling down

With the motor off, the gimbal can't steer. So the airframe must be passively
stable in **both** directions:

| Phase | Flow direction | What keeps it straight | Static margin (calibers) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boost (motor on) | nose-first | TVC + fins | +0.73 |
| Coast up (motor off, 2.6 s) | nose-first | **fins** | **+0.81** |
| Fall, airbrakes closed | tail-first | nothing: fins at the leading end destabilise | **−4.34** (tumbles) |
| Fall, airbrakes open (3.7 s) | tail-first | **top airbrakes** | **+0.51** |
| Landing burn | tail-first, slowing | TVC + airbrakes | (active) |

**Why airbrakes at the top work.**
- The rocket is tail-heavy: the motors, gimbal and legs are aft, putting the
  CG at 1.02 m of 1.52 m.
- A tail-heavy body falls like a shuttlecock only if there is drag behind it,
  at the top.
- Falcon 9 gets that from grid fins at the top of the stage. SLR-1 gets it
  from four petals that are part of the tube until apogee, then spring out
  to 90°.

They also set a steady **~18.5 m/s** fall. That is ideal for the F25W, and the
burn always starts from about the same state.

The CP model is component build-up:
- **Nose cone:** Barrowman ogive, CNα = 2, only when it leads the flow.
- **Blunt aft end:** CNα ≈ 1 when it leads.
- **Fins:** Barrowman, the same in both directions.
- **Airbrakes:** flat plates, C_d·S/A = 5.9 at 0.43 m.
- **Body:** viscous crossflow.

The model is in `sim/rocketsim/vehicle.py`. Background:
[Nakka](https://www.nakka-rocketry.net/RD_Appendix_B.html),
[OpenRocket tech doc](https://openrocket.sourceforge.net/techdoc.pdf).
OpenRocket assumes nose-first flight, so use it for mass, CG and drag only.

**The simulator's trade study** (Monte Carlo, 100-300 flights each):

| Configuration | Result |
|---|---|
| Finless, no airbrakes | tumbles on the coast up: 0% |
| Finless + airbrakes | tumbles on the coast up: 0% |
| Fins scaled to 85% + airbrakes | marginal coast: 50% clean, 44% tumble |
| **Full fins + airbrakes (baseline)** | **93% clean, 0 crashes** |

### Terminal velocity check

v_t = sqrt(2 m g / (ρ C_d A)):

- **Airbrakes closed:** C_A ≈ 1.07, A = 8.1e-3 m², m = 1.25 kg → ~49 m/s.
- **Airbrakes open:** C_d·A = 0.057 m² → **~19 m/s**. The simulator gives
  18.5 m/s at the fire point after a 48 m fall.

## 3.4 Top airbrake petals

```
   stowed (part of the tube)            open at apogee (springs, 90 deg stop)
   ===|=======|=========                ===|        |=========
      | petal |                            |petal   |
   ===|=======|=========                      \  (sticks out 125 mm into the airflow)
     hinge (forward edge)
```

- **Cut:** cut a 125 mm band of the body tube at 0.42-0.545 m into four 90°
  quadrants (~80 mm arc each). Each petal keeps the tube's curvature, so it
  sits flush when stowed.
- **Hinges:** a printed CF-nylon hinge on the forward edge of each petal, with
  a stainless pin.
- **Springs:** a torsion spring per petal, ~0.1 N·m preload.
- **Stop:** a printed stop at 90°. Make it stiff, because the petals take the
  full dynamic pressure at 19 m/s, about 220 Pa or 2.6 N per petal.
- **Latch:** a 3D-printed ring with hooks over the petals' aft edges, rotated
  by one micro servo (`SERVO_AIRBRAKE_LATCH`, pin 7).
- **Opening:** at apogee the servo turns the ring and the petals spring open
  in < 0.2 s.
- **Structure:** behind the petals sits a full coupler sleeve, so the airframe
  keeps its strength and the backup chute bay stays sealed.
- **Upgrade path:** servo-driven petals, or real 3D-printed grid fins, could
  *steer* the fall like Falcon 9. That needs 2-4 more servos.

## 3.5 Structure and materials

| Part | Material | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Body tube | LOC kraft 3.9 in (103 g/ft) | Reinforce the aft 6 in internally (coupler or CF sleeve) where the legs, fins and gimbal frame load it ([LOC tubes](https://locprecision.com/products/cardboard-airframes), [tube mass comparison](https://fabacademy.org/2025/labs/techworks/students/omar-albalbaki/Body%20Tubes%201c70e8ca7ada81a6b85ddbe39d7d69ba.html)) |
| Airbrake petals | Cut from the tube; edges sealed with thin CA; inner face stiffened with one layer of 2 oz fibreglass | |
| Nose | PETG, 0.8 mm walls, 15% gyroid | |
| Bulkheads, centering rings | 1/8 in birch ply, or printed PETG/CF-nylon | Motor-bay thrust bulkhead: ply + aluminium thrust plate |
| Gimbal | CF-nylon / PETG printed rings, **aluminium motor sleeves**, steel pins / small bearings | PLA softens at ~60 °C, so don't use it near the motor ([PETG vs PLA](https://www.makeitfrom.com/compare/Glycol-Modified-Polyethylene-Terephthalate-PETG-PET-G/Polylactic-Acid-PLA-Polylactide)) |
| Paddles | Machinable ceramic (alumina-silicate) or graphite on stainless arms | Inspect after every burn ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/one-mans-dream-to-build-a-working-spacex-style-model-rocket/)) |
| Fins | 1/8 in birch ply, epoxy fillets | Through-the-wall |
| Legs | 8 mm OD carbon tube, printed hinge clevises, TPU feet | See [docs/07](07-landing-legs.md) |
| Adhesive | 30-minute epoxy (or West System 105/206 + 406) for structure; high-temperature epoxy near the motors | |

## 3.6 Launch hardware

- **Pad:** a flat steel blast plate with a short guide or launch clamp. Clear
  this with the RSO ([docs/02](02-regulations-and-safety.md)).
- **Landing pad (optional):** a 2 x 2 m plywood sheet gives the feet and the
  lidar a flat, reflective target.
