# 6. Guidance, navigation and control

The same algorithms exist twice:
- `sim/rocketsim/guidance.py` (Python, used by the simulator), and
- `firmware/src/core/` (C++, flown).

`make -C firmware test` checks that the C++ reproduces the Python numbers
(`firmware/test/native/golden.h`, generated by `firmware/tools/gen_tables.py`)
and flies a full software-in-the-loop mission through the real C++ state
machine.

## 6.1 State machine

```
PAD_SAFE ──(key in, calibrated, vertical < 5°, continuity A & B)──> PAD_ARMED
PAD_ARMED ──(key out)──> PAD_SAFE
PAD_ARMED ──(axial accel > 2 g for 50 ms)──> BOOST          TVC on (ascent curve, 2.1° offset trim)
BOOST ──(axial accel < 0 for 25 ms, t > 0.3 s)──> COAST     gimbal centred
COAST ──(KF velocity < -0.5 m/s for 50 ms)──> DESCENT       drogue latch opens
DESCENT ──(guidance says FIRE and envelope OK)──> LANDING_BURN   both igniters, 1 s pulse
DESCENT ──(tilt > 35° below 70% of apogee) or (h < 15 m and outside envelope)──> ABORT_CHUTE
LANDING_BURN ──(no thrust 0.9 s after FIRE)──> ABORT_CHUTE
LANDING_BURN ──(h_feet <= 5 cm)──> TOUCHDOWN    paddles closed, drogue cut, attitude hold
TOUCHDOWN ──(burnout + 2 s)──> LANDED_SAFE      pyro disabled, log written to SD
```

## 6.2 Navigation (state estimation)

**Attitude.** A gyro-integrated quaternion, q̇ = ½ q ⊗ [0, ω − b].

- On the pad the vehicle is still, so the accelerometer gives the initial
  attitude (a 3 s average) and the gyro bias b.
- In flight the accelerometer is **not** a gravity reference. It reads thrust
  and drag, and 0 g in free fall. So attitude runs on the gyros alone for the
  ~15 s flight.
- A Teensy 4.1 + BMI088 project reports ~0.45° of drift over a 3.45 s burn
  ([RocketFC](https://github.com/trooper7652-commits/RocketFC)); the bias
  calibration matters.
- The Madgwick ([doi:10.1109/ICORR.2011.5975346](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICORR.2011.5975346))
  and Mahony ([doi:10.1109/TAC.2008.923738](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2008.923738))
  filters only help on the pad.

**Altitude and vertical speed.** A 3-state Kalman filter, x = [h, v, b_a]:

- **Predict** at 1 kHz with the world-frame vertical specific force,
  a = (q ⊗ f_body ⊗ q*)_z − g. Modelling the accelerometer bias b_a as a state
  matters. One study found that an unmodelled bias drove the velocity estimate
  through zero ~1 s early
  ([Rice thesis](https://repository.rice.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/0fbefe49-d831-4891-b83f-d8c2f05ce2f9/content)).
- **Barometer** update at 50 Hz, σ = 0.5 m (3 m during motor burns, because of
  plume and ram pressure). Outliers beyond 5σ are rejected.
- **Lidar** update when the range is < 10 m and the tilt is < 30°,
  h = r·cos θ − offset, σ = 6 cm. This makes the last metres precise.
- **Zero-velocity** updates on the pad and after landing.

**Horizontal velocity.** The world-frame accelerometer is integrated at 1 kHz
and corrected toward GPS NED velocity (gain 0.2 at 10 Hz). The GPS frame is
rotated into the attitude frame using the pad heading of fin 1
(`PAD_HEADING_DEG`).

## 6.3 TVC attitude control (dynamic-inversion PID)

A thrust T deflected by small angles (δ_y, δ_z) at a lever L aft of the CG,
with the motor offset e along body y, gives body torques

τ_y = L·T·sin δ_z,   τ_z = −L·T·sin δ_y − e·T.

The controller chooses a desired angular acceleration and inverts that relation
**using the current thrust**, so the loop bandwidth stays the same as thrust
rises and falls:

α_des = ω_n²·e_att − 2ζω_n·ω + K_i∫e_att,   sin δ_z = I·α_y / (L·T),
sin δ_y = −I·α_z / (L·T) − e/L.

- **Gains:** ω_n = 12 rad/s, ζ = 0.85, K_i = 4.
- **Attitude error:** e_att = x̂_body × û_cmd. This is the rotation that
  carries the body axis onto the commanded thrust direction. Roll is
  uncontrolled; working in the body frame handles it automatically.
- **Anti-windup:** conditional integration (no integrating while saturated);
  the integrator is reset at each ignition.
- **No thrust, no control:** the gimbal centres below 1 N.
- **Gimbal limit:** ±7°. Servo lag (30 ms) and a 400°/s rate limit are modelled
  in the sim.

Background reading: PID vs. LQR on finless TVC rockets
([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369325159_Thrust_Vector_Controller_Comparison_for_a_Finless_Rocket)),
the BPS/MathWorks Simulink model
([GitHub](https://github.com/mathworks/Modeling-TVCd-Rocket-in-Simulink)).

## 6.4 Landing guidance: throttle-ramp plan (the key algorithm)

**Vehicle model during the burn (vertical):**

ḣ = v,   v̇ = k(t)·s·T(t_b)·cos θ / m(t_b) + D(v)/m − g

- T(t_b) is the certified thrust curve.
- s is the thrust scale, learned in flight from the accelerometer.
- k ∈ [k_min, 1] is the paddle throttle.
- D is drag.

The equation is **linear in k**. Choose a ramp k(s) = k0 + k1·s over the time
left, τ = t_land − t_b, and require h(τ) = 0 and v(τ) = −v_td (0.5 m/s). With

A0 = ∫a ds,  A1 = ∫s·a ds,  B0 = ∫(τ−s)·a ds,  B1 = ∫s(τ−s)·a ds,
a(s) = s·T(t_b+s)·cos θ / m(t_b+s),

the two conditions become a **2x2 linear system**:

k0·A0 + k1·A1 = −v_td − v + gτ − ΔV_drag
k0·B0 + k1·B1 = −h − vτ + ½gτ² − ΔH_drag

It is solved with 40-point trapezoid integrals, roughly 0.1 ms on a Teensy.

**Touchdown window.** The touchdown burn clock t_land must lie in [t1, t2]:

- **t1:** the earliest time at which closed paddles can no longer lift the
  vehicle off its legs, k_min·T(t) < W. Landing earlier means a hop.
- **t2:** the last time at which there is still thrust to arrest the final
  descent, T(t) > 1.25·W.
- For a G40W at 1.33 kg and k_min = 0.25, the window is **[1.12 s, 1.94 s]**.
  With k_min = 0.40 it shrinks to [1.62 s, 1.94 s]. That is why paddle
  authority matters so much.

**Each re-plan (50 Hz):** try 9 values of t_land across the window, solve each,
score each by how far its k0 and k_end fall outside [k_min, 1] (plus a small
penalty for moving t_land), and command k = clip(k0).

**When to fire.** While falling, the computer predicts the state at ignition by
coasting the expected ignition delay under drogue drag. It then solves the plan
for mid-window landing.

- Far from the ground, the plan is "fall now, brake hard later" (k1 ≫ 0).
- As the vehicle descends, k1 falls monotonically.
- **FIRE when k1 ≤ 0**, i.e. when a constant-throttle burn would land
  mid-window. That point sits in the middle of the feasible ignition band, so
  scatter in the ignition delay in either direction is absorbed by the
  paddles.
- Nominal fire altitude at 22.7 m/s is **20.95 m**, the same in the Python and
  the C++.

**Terminal phase (h < 2 m).** Constant-deceleration braking,
a = g + (v² − v_td²)/(2h), turned into a throttle through the current thrust
estimate.

**Lateral.** Lean the *thrust* by φ = clip(−1.5·v_h/g, ±12°), fading to 0
below 1.5 m. The body then leans by φ minus the offset trim so that the trim
itself doesn't push the vehicle sideways. The cos θ loss is included in the
throttle plan.

This is a cut-down cousin of real powered-descent guidance. With a
non-throttleable solid motor and a narrow throttle band, a full convex solver
would be overkill. Further reading:

- Açıkmeşe & Ploen, convex programming for Mars powered descent, JGCD 2007,
  [doi:10.2514/1.27553](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.27553)
- Blackmore, Açıkmeşe & Scharf, minimum-landing-error PDG, JGCD 2010,
  [doi:10.2514/1.47202](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.47202)
- Scharf et al., G-FOLD flight tests on Masten Xombie,
  [doi:10.2514/1.G000399](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.G000399)
- Blackmore, "Autonomous Precision Landing of Space Rockets", NAE Frontiers 2016,
  [doi:10.17226/23659](https://doi.org/10.17226/23659)
- Malyuta et al., review of convex optimisation for spaceflight guidance,
  [arXiv:2108.02335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02335)

## 6.5 Simulation (sim/)

**The model.** Planar 3-DOF (x, z, pitch) with:

- the real thrust curves; mass and CG that change as propellant burns;
- component aerodynamics valid for nose-first and tail-first flow;
- a drogue and a backup chute;
- servo lag and rate limits; paddle-throttle lag;
- wind with a 1/7 power-law profile plus gusts;
- sensor noise and biases;
- ground contact with hop detection.

The flight computer in the loop is the Python twin of the firmware.

**Nominal flight** (`run_nominal.py`):

**Monte Carlo** (`run_montecarlo.py`, 300 flights). Dispersions:

- ignition delay 0.30 ± 0.08 s
- impulse ±3%, burn time ±3%
- k_min 0.25 ± 0.03, throttle gain ±3%
- C_d ±10%, mass ±2%
- wind 0-4 m/s
- launch tilt 1°, thrust misalignment 0.25°
- altitude bias 15 cm, gyro bias 0.05°/s

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Clean landings (≤ 2.5 m/s vertical, ≤ 1.5 m/s lateral, ≤ 10° tilt, hop ≤ 0.3 m) | **270 / 300 (90%)** - the other 30 are hard landings; **0 crashes** |
| Vertical touchdown | mean 1.48 m/s, p95 2.32 m/s, max 2.79 m/s |
| Lateral touchdown | p5-p95 −1.13 to +1.41 m/s (the main failure driver) |
| Apogee | mean 153 m; p5-p95 138-169 m |
| Wind 0-2 m/s | **100%** clean |
| Wind 2-3 m/s | 92% |
| Wind 3-4 m/s | 72% → **fly landing attempts below 2.5 m/s** |

![Monte Carlo touchdown](img/mc_touchdown.png)
![Success vs wind](img/mc_wind.png)

**What the simulation does not capture:**

- 3-D coupling and roll;
- drogue pendulum dynamics beyond a rigid-attachment approximation;
- plume effects on the paddles;
- lidar dropouts;
- structural flexibility.

Treat the 90% as "the design closes on paper with these assumptions", not as
a prediction. **Re-run the Monte Carlo with your measured ignition delay,
k_min, mass and inertia.**

## 6.6 Tuning order

1. Gimbal linkage calibration, then IMU-to-body mapping (`IMU_MAP`). Check
   signs on the bench: tilt the rocket by hand and watch the gimbal.
2. Mass, CG and inertia measured → `params.h` and `designs.py`.
3. Static fires → ignition delay, thrust scale, k_min → `params.h`, then re-run
   the Monte Carlo.
4. Attitude gains: keep ω_n ≤ ~1/3 of the gimbal servo's bandwidth (~25 Hz for
   BPS-class mounts, [BPS](https://bps.space/products/thrust-vector-control)).
   Verify on a tethered static fire (motor burning, vehicle free to pivot).

## 6.7 Safety interlocks

| Layer | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Hardware | Arm key physically opens the pyro rail; 10 kΩ gate pull-downs; 10 A fuse |
| Pad arming | `PAD_ARMED` requires the key, pad calibration done, tilt < 5°, continuity on both igniters, battery > 7.4 V |
| Disarm | Pulling the key returns to `PAD_SAFE` and disables pyro |
| Fire envelope | tilt < 20°, h_feet > 5 m, −35 < v_z < −8 m/s, below the 120 m gate, in `DESCENT` only |
| One shot | The HAL fires one 1 s pulse per boot |
| Abort | No ignition 0.9 s after FIRE, tilt > 35°, or outside the envelope below 15 m → main chute; landing guidance off; hang-fire handling holds the nozzle down |
| After landing | `LANDED_SAFE` disables pyro and writes the log |
